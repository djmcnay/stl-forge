## Rear sketch stage

The rear elevation is being redrawn as a black-and-white CAD tracing outline from
`references/defender/rear.png` with the local Qwen Image 2.1 Turbo edit runtime,
prompt frozen in `references/defender/rear-sketch-prompt.txt`.

```bash
/Users/djmcnay/.local/bin/mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit \
  --model ~/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--OsaurusAI--Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo-mflux-8bit/snapshots/b03f3e21956b3f492f59f16299ec901c237bbc4e \
  --base-model qwen-image-2.1-turbo --quantize 8 --image-paths references/defender/rear.png \
  --prompt-file references/defender/rear-sketch-prompt.txt --width 1024 --height 1024 \
  --steps 8 --seed 99 --guidance 1 --low-ram --output out.png

python3 tools/trace_sketch.py out.png out-1bit.png out.svg 0.5 200
```

`trace_sketch.py` thresholds the render, traces closed contours on pixel cracks and
simplifies them, so the SVG is a pixel-exact (IoU 1.0) vector version of the 1-bit
drawing and imports as real sketch geometry rather than a raster canvas.

**Approved render: seed 42 with `rear-sketch-prompt.txt`.** The principal reviewed
the candidates and selected this one, so it is the frozen sketch for the rear bead.
It keeps the mud flaps and does not draw the road wheels; that was the accepted
trade for the cleanest body, spare wheel and lamp geometry. Do not re-roll the
prompt or regenerate the sketch unless the principal asks.

Check the alignment against the photo with the CPU-only overlay tool:

```bash
python3 tools/overlay_trace.py references/defender/rear.png <sketch.png> \
    references/defender/rear-trace-overlay.png
```

It fits uniform scale plus translation by chamfer matching and reports the residual.
Current fit: scale 0.690, offset (-22, 11), mean edge distance 1.53 px, with 86.9%
of sketch ink within 3 px of a photo edge.

Differences the overlay shows, for whoever traces the bead:

- Roof: the sketch roof is a continuous dome; the photo has a flatter roof with a
  distinct gutter step. Widen and flatten the roof line when tracing.
- Rear door glass: the sketch window is slightly taller than the photo's opening.
- Mud flaps: the sketch's left flap is narrower than the photo's.
- The sketch bumper line sits slightly above the photo's cross-member.

Both sides agree closely on the body width, spare wheel (tyre, rim, hub and nut
circle), Alpine windows and the corner lamp circles. The 1024 px render is a
tracing guide only; final bead dimensions come from measurement, not the render.
The photo-to-sketch direction scores lower (47.4%) only because the photo contains
fine detail the sketch deliberately omits - tread, badges, bolts and interior.

## Rear part map stage

The pink trace is the authority for the rear silhouette.  It is **not** redrawn,
re-traced or "cleaned up": `tools/part_map.py` takes the ink exactly as it is,
decides which part each ink pixel belongs to, and repaints it in that part's
colour.  The delivered PNG is the approved trace pixel for pixel, plus one small
addition the principal asked for (see `arch_join` below): the tool asserts that
every source ink pixel is reproduced and reports any ink beyond the trace, so a
redraw cannot creep back in.

```bash
python3 projects/giant-beads/tools/part_map.py             # recolour + JSON
python3 projects/giant-beads/tools/part_map.py --check -v  # ownership report
```

Outputs, both committed:

- `references/defender/rear-parts-by-colour.png` - the trace, recoloured, with a
  legend grouped by treatment level (silhouette / raised / lights / etched /
  proposed delete / omit).
- `references/defender/rear-parts.json` - per part, the ink traced into closed
  loops (even-odd fill, overlay pixels).  This is the geometry the SCAD rebuild
  must use, not a re-measurement.

The part model in `tools/part_map.py` (`PARTS`) is a set of **proximity probes**
used only to decide ownership; it is never drawn.  A `bias` per part lets a
probe keep the stroke it sits on when a neighbour is closer (the doubled panel
edges are ~12-16 px apart).

Frame and scale:

- Trace canvas is 1316 x 1560 px, origin top-left, y down, body centre `CX = 660`.
- The widest point of the body is clipped by the canvas edge, so the full
  1316 px is the 120 mm bead width: **1 px = 0.0912 mm**.
- Spare wheel centre (700, 713): tyre 171.5/300, rim 139/155, rim holes 12 at
  r=126 (30 deg spacing), dish 90 (dashed in the trace) and 108, wheel nuts 5 at
  r=63, hub 43, hole radius 9.5, nut radius 9.  Radii measured along eight rays.

Treatment agreed by the principal:

| level | colour | parts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| silhouette | navy `#1B3B6F` | body outline; tyres at the bottom (L + R); `bumper_bottom`; `arch_join`; `hip_join` |
| etched (one depth, both faces) | purple `#6C3483` | `bumper_top` (top edge and end stubs); roof gutter; rear door frame; rear door window; alpine quarter windows; number plate; spare rim holes (8); spare wheel nuts (5); spare hub circle |
| raised: lights | red `#D64541` | six round lamps |
| raised: spare tyre | green `#0E7A55` | spare tyre outer edge (r=300) |
| raised: steel wheel | teal `#148F77` | steel wheel outer edge (r=171.5) |
| raised: waist seam | light green `#7DCE7A` | waist capping seams |
| proposed delete | grey `#98A0A5` | reflector strips; spare dish circles; spare steel wheel trim |
| omit | grey `#BFC5C9` | door hinges; door latch/handle |

Every purple etch is one depth.  The four raised groups each take their own
height.  The cross member sits flush with the car, so it is not a raised part:
its bottom edge is silhouette and its top edge (plus the short end stubs) is an
etch.

Edits to the trace, all requested by the principal and recorded in
`rear-parts.json` under `edits`:

- removed: the roof's inner panel line, so the roof is one outline;
- removed: the door bottom rail jambs, so the etch runs right round the door;
- removed: the traced 12 rim holes, replaced by 8 larger ones at r=126 on 45 deg
  spacing (radius 13 px, i.e. 2.4 mm across);
- added: two short strips closing the wheel-arch corners (x=36.5, x=1279.5,
  y 1077-1100);
- added: two straight verticals closing the body "hips" down to the sill
  (x=1, y 901-1080 and x=1314, y 924-1080), because the trace runs off the
  canvas at the widest point.

Still open:

1. Every panel edge in the trace is doubled (an outer and an inner stroke
   12-16 px apart).  Only the roof's pair has been collapsed so far; the door,
   the two windows and the plate still carry both strokes, which on the bead are
   two grooves ~1.1 mm apart.  Same treatment for those if wanted.
2. The bumper's end stubs stop around y=1072 in the trace, short of the bumper's
   bottom edge at y=1131.
3. The spare wheel is 40 px (3.7 mm) right of the body centre line; left as
   traced.
4. The tyre has no tread in the trace, only the two circles.

Once a part list is agreed, `bead_defender_rear.scad` is rebuilt from
`rear-parts.json`: silhouette parts make the extruded profile, raised parts get
their own positive level, etched parts become grooves on both faces.

## Bead sketch (clean base)

The part map records decisions; it is not the drawing to build from.  The bead
base is `references/defender/rear-bead-sketch.png`, produced by

```bash
python3 projects/giant-beads/tools/bead_sketch.py            # build + fit report
python3 projects/giant-beads/tools/bead_sketch.py --check    # fit report only
```

The whole path - trace, part map, sign-off sheet, clean sketch, build JSON - is
described once in [tools/README.md](tools/README.md), along with how to add the
next elevation.  Every per-view number lives in `tools/views/<view>.py`; the
engines only know mechanism, so the side and the front are a new view module and
a `--view` flag, not a new script.

It is derived from the trace, not redrawn:

- The **silhouette comes out of the trace ink**.  The outline strokes are closed,
  filled and re-contoured, so the profile is the principal's shape even where the
  trace had gaps, and gaps that trapped the wheel-arch flares are filled with the
  measured wedge between arch, hip line and flank.
- Every **internal feature is one stroke**.  The trace draws most panel edges
  twice, 12-16 px apart (1.1-1.5 mm on the bead, i.e. two grooves that would print
  as one mush); the clean sketch uses the mid position once.
- **Deleted and omitted parts are absent**: no reflectors, no dish circles, no
  wheel trim, no hinges, no latch, no door bottom rail.
- The **number plate is pulled clear of the door**: its right edge sits at x=272
  against the door's left edge at 324, a 4.8 mm land, where the trace had 2.6 mm.

`references/defender/rear-bead-sketch-overlay.png` is the same lines drawn over
`rear.png`.  The trace frame is the photograph at 2x with no offset, so the
overlay needs no fitting: any misregistration visible on it is in the trace, not
in the transcription.  Comparing the two shows the trace is a few percent larger
than the photograph - the spare tyre and the bottom corner blocks most of all -
because the generated sketch stretched the car to fill its canvas.

Geometry is in `references/defender/rear-bead-sketch.json`: the silhouette as a
closed polygon plus 17 parts as plain primitives (polyline, rounded rectangle,
circle, ring array).  That file, not the PNG, is what the SCAD template consumes.
Scale is set from the finished silhouette, so the bead is exactly 120 mm across:
1 px = 0.0930 mm.

Levels on the sheet:

| level | what it means |
| --- | --- |
| silhouette | the bead profile |
| etched | one depth, engraved on both faces; eleven parts |
| raised: lights | semi-domes |
| raised: spare tyre | its own height |
| raised: steel wheel | its own height, inside the tyre |
| raised: waist seam | its own height |

Fit against the trace ink: mean 2.7 px (0.25 mm), with the 95th percentile at
16 px because the doubled strokes the sketch collapses sit exactly that far away.

Open points carried over:

1. The wheel-arch flare is now part of the profile, so the bead widens by ~4 mm
   over the rear wheels - that is what the trace shows at the canvas edge.  If a
   straight-sided body is preferred, drop `FLARE_WEDGES` and the flank becomes
   the outline.
2. The rear window's bottom edge is still drawn across the tyre; on the bead the
   raised tyre simply covers that part of the groove.
3. The spare is 3.8 mm right of the body centre, as traced.

## Rear bead build (v2)

The sketch is now a solid. `templates/bead_defender_rear_v2.scad` builds the
bead from `templates/bead_defender_rear_data.scad`, which
`tools/bead_scad.py` generates from `rear-bead-sketch.json`. The template holds
no geometry: regenerate the data after any sketch change and commit it with the
template, or `tests/test_bead_data.py` fails as stale.

Parameters, as specified: `width` and `height` from the family (`height =
width / 2`, so 120 x 60 - the 30 mm this file used to quote was a hardcode in
the superseded pair, now fixed there too), `etch_depth` from the family value
`0.6 * detail_relief`, and a **1 mm** edge fillet - tight on purpose, so the
boxy Defender read survives.  The pad roundover and cove follow the fillet
down, so all the rolling on the face is one radius. The raised levels are fractions of the bead
thickness:

| level | height | at H = 60 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| lights (domes) | height / 30 | 2.0 mm |
| spare tyre | height / 10 | 6.0 mm |
| steel wheel | height / 15 | 4.0 mm |
| waist seam | height / 30 | 2.0 mm |

Every raised pad has a 1 mm roundover on its top edge and a 1 mm cove where it
meets the face.

**The road wheels** are a separate solid each, because on the real car they sit
ahead of the rear panel. The chassis line runs across the bottom of the body
(derived from the trace: the profile below it *is* the two wheels, and comes
back as two loops, not one band). Each wheel is sunk `height / 20` (3 mm) below
the face.

**The wheel is a curved rectangle in the face and a wheel in profile.** Two
mistakes are worth remembering:

- *Out of the face* a tyre is a tread band - near-straight sides, the traced
  round bottom. Reworking the outline into a semicircle made it read as a mud
  flap, and it broke the rule that the trace is the geometry. The outline is
  now exactly the trace again, checked by `test_the_outline_is_the_trace`.
- *The shelf* was the recess floor. Every shape that dipped inside the wheel's
  footprint exposed that floor as a horizontal face running the length of each
  leg, opening onto the bead's own side wall - the thing the principal crossed
  out, three times, because four renders of it did not show it clearly.

**What the wheel is now.** The footprint itself, from the plate up, stopping
`tread_low` below the face, with its edge rolled over by a shoulder of the same
radius as the recess. The tread is flat and never comes closer than that
shoulder to the leg's wall, so there is no flat face at the wall and no shelf;
and the contour is a quarter round from the vertical side wall into the tread -
the shape the principal drew on the profile, and the only curvature a 3 mm
recess can carry.

`test_no_flat_shelf_at_the_wheel` renders the bead and checks this on the mesh
rather than on a picture: every horizontal face at the tread height must be at
least part of a shoulder in from the footprint boundary. It is deliberately
loose - the shoulder's slices leave shallow terraces - because the fault it
guards is a flat face that reaches the wall, which sits at zero.

The dome is cut by the wheel's footprint and stops 0.3 mm inside it, because a
round surface tangent to the side wall meets it in a knife edge. The footprint
is used for the pocket cut too, offset 0.25 mm outwards: on the outer edge the
footprint *is* the silhouette, and subtracting a wall from a coincident wall
leaves the sliver that showed up as a flake above the wheel.

Three attempts stand between the sketch and this, worth knowing about:
a cylinder crowned across the width alone (a barrel, round in the wrong
plane), strips of shrinking prisms (same shape, but the steps banded in the
render), and the semicircular tips above.

Earlier faults, all fixed and covered by tests:

- the cove profile was inverted, so instead of a fillet it built a thin ledge
  floating over each wheel - the "artifact above the wheels";
- the cove was clipped to the wheel's outline but it reaches further in than the
  outline allows, so it grew a lip out past the wheel;
- the wheel had no rollover of its own, so it stayed a flat-topped box.

**The two faces are mirror images in line.** The bead is viewed from the front
on one side and from behind on the other, so the build-plate etch is mirrored in
x: the number plate stays on the same hand as the raised face, and the offset
spare offsets the other way.

**Detail is nudged inboard to clear the face fillet.** The edge fillet pulls the
flat face in all the way round, so anything drawn tighter than that to the
outline overhangs the rounded shoulder. `bead_scad.py` measures each raised dome
and each etched box against the outline and slides it towards the centre until
it clears:

- an **etched box** moves by the smallest amount that clears the fillet plus
  half again.  At a 1 mm fillet the alpine windows (1.92 / 2.00 mm clear) already
  fit, so they stay exactly where the sketch drew them;
- a **lamp** sat too close moves by **3 mm** - the principal's figure - or
  further if the fillet needs it.  On the rear that is the two upper lamps; the
  four lower and mid lamps are already clear and are untouched.

The report prints every move, so a future change to the fillet shows up as a
different list rather than a silent shift.

The tyre is an annulus (outer edge to the steel wheel's edge), so the wheel sits
as a disc inside it and the two steps read. The bumper cross member stays flush
with the car, so only those four levels are raised. Etches are one depth on both
faces; the wheel detail (8 rim holes, 5 nuts, hub) is cut at the raised wheel's
own height so it is not buried.

**Nothing protrudes from the bottom.** The bead is printed on that face, so it
is flat apart from grooves: the raised levels are repeated there as flat etch
outlines (tyre and wheel rings, seam lines, lamp circles) instead of relief.

**The outline is de-staircased.** The trace is raster, so a flank that flares
gently comes back as a staircase of ~1 mm steps - visible as a hard line beside
the rear lamps. `bead_scad.py` smooths the ring, simplifies it twice at 0.2 mm,
and then scales it about its own centre so the bead is still exactly 120 mm
across (the smoothing pulls the extremes in by ~0.6 mm, and the width is a
family parameter). A test fails if any short edge pair still forms a step.

One CAD-only change to the sketch: `bead_scad.py` drops profile points within
0.2 mm of their neighbours' chord. The sketch is traced on pixel cracks, and the
sub-pixel wobble came out of the extruder as a faceted wall - visible in preview,
invisible in print. The outline moves by at most 0.2 mm, a fifth of the etch
width.

Renders: `python3 utilities/forge.py --presets projects/giant-beads/presets.json
--only bead_defender_rear_v2 --out .tmp/out`. Manifold, 178k facets, one solid,
positive volume; 1.4 s for the CGAL render.

Seen in the first build, for the principal to rule on:

1. The raised tyre lands on top of the door's bottom edge and the rear window's
   bottom edge, because the trace draws the tyre overlapping them. The etch
   runs into the tyre and stops - physically honest (the wheel is bolted over
   the door) but it reads as a broken line in a flat front view. Options: etch
   the lines across the raised tyre, or shrink the tyre pad.
2. The string hole runs through the width at mid-thickness, so it passes through
   the spare wheel's raised disc. That is the family convention and it leaves
   7 mm of material above the bore, but the hole could be moved if it looks
   wrong on the print.
3. The roof gutter etch runs close to the fillet band at the very top of the
   roof. At a 1 mm fillet it clears, but the groove is shallower than the rest at
   its extreme ends where the roof curve pulls in.
4. `bead_defender_rear.scad` (the hand-drawn first pass) and the app panel still
   point at the old geometry until v2 is approved. Its height now follows the
   family rule like v2's does; expect the app's defender beads to render 60 mm
   thick rather than 30.
5. The wheel step is 3 mm deep with a 6 mm cove, so the cove is a partial arc
   and the wheel meets the chassis line at a slight angle rather than
   tangentially. Deepen `tread_low` if you want it tangent. The wheel's own
   roundover is 3 mm; widen it if the tyre should look fatter.

## Trace conventions

- Green: outer body silhouette (wheels included, so the wheel outlines sit inside it).
- Orange: visible side door seam, handle and hinges. The 90 has one side door; the rear quarter is fixed bodywork.
- Cyan: front door window, rear side window, alpine roof window and window divisions.
- Yellow: road-wheel tyres, rims, hubs and wheel nuts.
- Amber: spare wheel behind the rear body (full tyre, rim and hub, dashed where hidden).
- Included: side step, drawn as its own separate outline below the body sill.
- Omitted on purpose: wing mirror, mudflaps, exhaust.
